# Massachusetts residential lease reference pack

This short reference pack summarizes selected state-law text for educational lease review. It is not legal advice, a complete statement of Massachusetts law, or a determination that a lease term is enforceable. The model may cite only the IDs below. A clause with no matching entry should not be given a state-law citation.

Verified against the official Massachusetts Legislature pages on 2026-11-15. The pack was reviewed with AI assistance and has not been reviewed by a Massachusetts attorney. See [VERIFICATION_LOG.md](./VERIFICATION_LOG.md).

## L1 — Amounts due at the start of a tenancy

**Rule summary:** For a residential tenancy, M.G.L. c. 186, § 15B(1)(b) limits what a lessor or the lessor's agent may require at or before move-in. The listed amounts include first month's rent, last month's rent at the same rate, a security deposit no greater than one month's rent subject to the statute's conditions, and the purchase and installation cost of a key and lock. The text also describes a possible fee in place of a security deposit if regulations authorize one and statutory safeguards are met. Do not treat every labeled “fee” as automatically unlawful: ask what it covers and whether another rule applies.

**Scope:** Residential real property. This summary does not decide whether a fee is optional, separately contracted, or covered by an authorized statutory program.

**Official source:** M.G.L. c. 186, § 15B(1)(b), [official text](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleI/Chapter186/Section15B).

## L2 — Security-deposit receipts, account, interest, and records

**Rule summary:** A residential security deposit must be held in a separate, interest-bearing account at a bank in Massachusetts. The statute requires a receipt with the bank's name and location, deposit amount, and account number within 30 days. It also sets interest rules, including annual interest after a deposit has been held for a year, and requires records about damage, repairs, and deposit transactions. The details and deadlines differ by requirement. A lease review should flag unclear or inconsistent language as a question, not conclude from the lease alone that the landlord complied or failed to comply.

**Scope:** Residential security deposits. The statutory conditions and exceptions apply.

**Official source:** M.G.L. c. 186, § 15B, [official text](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleI/Chapter186/Section15B).

## L3 — Statement of condition

**Rule summary:** When a lessor accepts a residential security deposit, § 15B(2)(c) requires a separate written statement describing the premises' present condition and existing damage. The lessor must provide it upon receiving the deposit or within 10 days after the tenancy starts, whichever is later. The tenant has 15 days after getting the statement or moving in, whichever is later, to return a signed list of disagreements. If the tenant submits a list, the lessor has 15 days to return a copy with agreement or a clear disagreement. The model cannot know whether a separate statement was delivered outside the lease.

**Scope:** Applies when a security deposit is accepted. A lease that does not mention a statement does not prove that none will be provided.

**Official source:** M.G.L. c. 186, § 15B(2)(c), [official text](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleI/Chapter186/Section15B).

## L4 — Return and deductions from a security deposit

**Rule summary:** The lessor generally must return the deposit or its balance within 30 days after the tenancy ends. Section 15B lists limited deductions, including certain unpaid rent or water charges, a qualifying tax increase, and reasonable repair costs for tenant-caused damage beyond ordinary wear and tear. For damage deductions, the lessor must provide a detailed sworn itemization and written cost evidence within that period. The statute bars deductions for purposes outside its list. A model should not decide whether a particular damage claim or deduction is valid without the facts and records.

**Scope:** Residential security deposits; statutory details and exceptions control.

**Official source:** M.G.L. c. 186, § 15B, [official text](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleI/Chapter186/Section15B).

## L5 — Remedies for specified deposit violations

**Rule summary:** Section 15B provides remedies for certain failures involving security deposits, including enhanced damages in specified circumstances. Which remedy applies depends on the exact statutory violation and facts. Do not predict a court award from lease wording alone.

**Scope:** Only the violations and remedies described in § 15B; this summary is not a claim that every deposit dispute results in multiple damages.

**Official source:** M.G.L. c. 186, § 15B, [official text](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleI/Chapter186/Section15B).

## L6 — Timing of rent penalties

**Rule summary:** Section 15B(1)(c) says a lease or rental agreement may not impose interest or a penalty for failure to pay rent until 30 days after the rent is due. A clause setting a charge earlier than that may be worth asking about. This provision does not resolve every charge that might be described as a fee.

**Scope:** The text concerns interest or penalties for failure to pay rent.

**Official source:** M.G.L. c. 186, § 15B(1)(c), [official text](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleI/Chapter186/Section15B).

## L7 — Entry, quiet enjoyment, and services

**Rule summary:** Section 15B(1)(a) restricts lease terms allowing a lessor to enter before a lease ends. It allows entry to inspect, make repairs, or show the premises to a prospective tenant, purchaser, mortgagee, or agent. It also lists entry under a court order, when the premises appear abandoned, or for a damage inspection during the final 30 days of a tenancy or after a termination notice. Section 14 addresses interference with quiet enjoyment and specified services in residential premises. Neither section establishes one general advance-notice period for every permitted entry.

**Scope:** Section 14 has statutory coverage and exceptions, including for some premises and service interruptions. Section 15B(1)(a) concerns entry before the termination date of a lease.

**Official sources:** M.G.L. c. 186, [§ 14](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleI/Chapter186/Section14) and [§ 15B](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleI/Chapter186/Section15B).

## L9 — Specific non-waivable protections

**Rule summary:** Sections 14 and 18 state that waivers of the provisions they describe are void or unenforceable, subject to the exceptions written into the statutes. This does not mean that every lease term or every tenant protection is non-waivable.

**Scope:** Only the specific statutory provisions and exceptions stated in §§ 14 and 18.

**Official sources:** M.G.L. c. 186, [§ 14](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleI/Chapter186/Section14) and [§ 18](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleI/Chapter186/Section18).

## L10 — Claims and defenses in certain possession cases

**Rule summary:** M.G.L. c. 239, § 8A allows specified claims or defenses relating to the rental property, tenancy, or occupancy in certain possession actions. When a claim concerns the condition of a property or services, the section includes additional conditions and procedures. It does not give general advice to withhold rent or predict the result of a case.

**Scope:** Specified possession actions and circumstances described in § 8A. It is not a complete habitability standard; separate codes and rules may apply.

**Official source:** M.G.L. c. 239, § 8A, [official text](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartIII/TitleIII/Chapter239/Section8A).

## L11 — Retaliation for specified tenant activity

**Rule summary:** Section 18 addresses threats or reprisals against tenants for specified actions, including certain legal proceedings, reports of suspected housing violations, written complaints, and tenant-organization activity. It describes damages and a rebuttable presumption for certain landlord actions within six months. Whether it applies depends on the activity, timing, and other facts.

**Scope:** Only the activities, actions, time period, and conditions stated in the statute.

**Official source:** M.G.L. c. 186, § 18, [official text](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleI/Chapter186/Section18).

## L15 — Interest on last month's rent paid in advance

**Rule summary:** Section 15B(2)(a) requires a receipt and addresses interest on rent paid in advance for the last month of a tenancy. Interest is generally 5% per year or the lower amount received from the bank. The provision describes annual payment, a forwarding-address notice, and payment of accrued interest after a tenancy ends; interest does not accrue during the month for which the advance rent is used. It does not apply to ordinary monthly rent paid when due.

**Scope:** Last month's rent collected in advance; separate statutory terms apply to security deposits.

**Official source:** M.G.L. c. 186, § 15B(2)(a), [official text](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleI/Chapter186/Section15B).

## L18 — Notice to end an estate at will

**Rule summary:** Section 12 describes notice to determine an estate at will. It generally ties the notice period to the rent-payment interval or 30 days, whichever is longer, and includes a separate rule for nonpayment of rent. This is not a general termination rule for every fixed-term lease.

**Scope:** Estates at will as defined by the statute. Do not apply it automatically to fixed-term leases or other housing arrangements.

**Official source:** M.G.L. c. 186, § 12, [official text](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleI/Chapter186/Section12).

## L19 — Specified residential services

**Rule summary:** Section 14 addresses a landlord's intentional failure to provide certain services when required by law or agreement, interference with another's provision of utilities, transfer of utility-payment responsibility without the occupant's knowledge or consent, and interference with quiet enjoyment. The statute also states exceptions and remedies.

**Scope:** Only the services, premises, and circumstances described in § 14. Separate statutes and regulations may govern particular utilities.

**Official source:** M.G.L. c. 186, § 14, [official text](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleI/Chapter186/Section14).
